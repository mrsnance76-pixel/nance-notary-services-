Today: acknowledgment versus jurat — the two notarial acts you'll use most, and the one people mix up the most.

An acknowledgment answers one question: is this your signature? The signer declares that the signature on the document is theirs. No oath is given. And here's the part people miss: the document can already be signed before they arrive. The act is the acknowledgment — not watching them sign.

A jurat is different. It answers: do you swear this is true? You administer an oath or affirmation, and the signer must sign the document in your presence — in front of you, not before they walk in.

Remember it this way. Acknowledgment: is this your signature? Jurat: do you swear this is true?

Whichever one you're doing, the signer must appear before you — in person, or through a properly authorized online notarization. An ordinary video call is not a valid notarization in Florida.

And your certificate must match the act: it says either sworn or acknowledged, along with the date, the signer's name, how you identified them, and your seal.
